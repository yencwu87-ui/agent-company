# The Line (treatment)

A film about the Agent Company World, in the Low-poly Isometric Island style. English, American narrator, burned-in subtitles, 75 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps, −14 LUFS.

Brief (owner, 20 Nov 2026): "no american voice over", "give it some context". Defaults taken where she left a gap: this style, a British female voice, no storyboard review.

**What this film is and is not.** It is an illustration of the idea, with context. It is not a recording of the product. The recorded run on a real project is `docs/media/world-demo.mp4` in the agent-company repo, and the end card says so. Narration avoids the claim words that this repo's claims check refuses, and says only what was built and tested.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One journey.** An empty platform; six stations rise one by one; the line runs. Clear, but it is the style's cliché (things grow from nothing) and it has no problem to solve, so no context.
2. **Before and after, with a refusal.** Open in the middle of a mess: loose agents roam a bare platform at dusk and work falls off the edge. Then stations land in order, each lamp lit only when the one before it is lit. Midway a crate tries to jump the queue and the gate stays shut. End on one final piece: the seal.
3. **Erosion.** Start with the finished line and take stations away to show what breaks. Clever, but it ends on failure and spends the whole film on the negative.

**Chosen: 2.** It gives the context she asked for in the first ten seconds, has a real turn (the refusal), and its ending is a single close piece, not a pull-back.

## 2. Logline and arc

Putting agents to work feels like trial and error, until the work is given a line with six stations that only open in order.

- **Setup (dusk):** loose agents, dropped work, no record.
- **Turn:** stations land one by one; a crate that tries to skip the Forge is refused at the gate.
- **Ending (dawn):** the line runs, and one seal drops onto the vault. Done means done.

## 3. Benchmark

- A tabletop logistics-game trailer (conveyor and factory builders in general): **learn** how a lateral move along a production line reads as a list, and how one refused item teaches a rule faster than narration. **Do not take** any game's machines, UI, palette or sounds.
- Airport departure boards and railway signalling: **learn** lamps that change one at a time as the grammar for "this may now proceed". **Do not take** any real operator's signage or type.

## 6. Cue map

- **Tempo:** 96 bpm throughout; acceleration is by density.
- **Scale:** D major pentatonic (D E F♯ A B), so any order is consonant.
- **Section A (bars 1–4), "loose":** low drone on D, wind bed, sparse woodblock off the beat. No ostinato. Narration lines 1–2.
- **Silence 1:** 8.75–10.0, drone only, music bus to zero for the last half beat.
- **Section B (bars 5–19), "the line":** every landing is a marimba or kalimba note. A soft kalimba ostinato enters at bar 7 and thickens at bar 10. Forge pieces play D4 E4 F♯4 A4 B4 D5: the motif. Narration lines 3–7.
- **Silence 2:** bar 14 beat 3 to bar 15 beat 2, ostinato thinned to nothing; the first sound back is the Forge lamp (bell, D5).
- **Section C (bars 20–25), "running":** ostinato full, shaker, vibraphone pad; motif replayed as crates pass each station; seal lands on a bell with a low D. Lines 8–9.
- **Section D (bars 26–30):** pad decays; one celesta D at the title.
- **Subtitles** appear with their spoken line and never cross a cut onto a title.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Subtitles: Quicksand 600, warm white with a soft dark-blue shadow, centred low, no box, a small flat-shaded hex icon beside the line whose top colour follows the time of day.
- Title: Josefin Sans caps, wide tracking, letters rising one by one with overshoot: "AGENT COMPANY WORLD". Under it, small: "python3 agent_company.py launch". Bottom, smaller: "An illustration. The recorded run on a real project is in docs/WORLD-GUIDE.md".

## Narration (American female; first cut was British, changed at the owner's request)

1. Putting an AI agent to work still feels like trial and error.
2. Work goes missing, and nobody can show what was done.
3. Agent Company gives the work a line, with six stations.
4. First a project. Then a team, with one job each.
5. Then the harness: six limits, fitted with one click.
6. Nothing skips ahead. A station opens only when the one before it is done.
7. Written orders go in, and one coordinator starts the line.
8. The screen shows only what is really in your repo.
9. Then the proof is sealed. Merging stays with you.

## As built (20 Nov 2026)

- **65 s, 26 bars**, not 75 s: the build machine had no GPU, so the film was shortened and rendered at 1280×720 with no supersampling. `sh build.sh` renders 1920×1080 with 2× supersampling.
- Beat sheet as built is in `story.js` (`T`): arrivals from bar 5, the six harness pieces in bars 8 to 12 with the sixth held back until after the refusal, the gate in bars 11 to 13, the line running from bar 17, the seal on bar 21, the title on bar 23.
- The gate shot is seen from the other side (azimuth 316 to 324°) so the waiting crate is in front of the bar, not hidden behind it.
- **Second round (owner's notes):** a soft dark band now sits behind the subtitles whenever the scene is bright (lines 1, 2, 8 and 9 were hard to read); the narrator changed from British (bf_emma) to American (af_heart). 564 frames were re-rendered and spliced in.
- **Known flaws:** the narration was not checked by speech-to-text (the model could not be downloaded); loudness was mastered with ffmpeg 6.1 by hand (−14.1 LUFS measured) because `mux.sh` needs ffmpeg 7.
